# Review Policy

## Review Axes

Every major contribution is reviewed across:

### 1. Technical correctness
Are mechanisms, APIs, registers, code, and diagrams correct?

### 2. Evidence quality
Are important claims backed by appropriate sources?

### 3. Teaching order
Do prerequisites exist before advanced concepts are introduced?

### 4. Mental model quality
Will the explanation create a durable and accurate model?

### 5. Reproducibility
Can the lab be rebuilt on the stated environment?

### 6. Debug value
Does the learner practice diagnosis, not only happy-path execution?

### 7. Version integrity
Are kernel/toolchain/RTOS/SoC versions explicit and consistent?

### 8. Career relevance
Does the depth match the current target role or enable later high-value topics?

### 9. Resource quality
Does the chapter use strong primary sources, suitable open-source projects, and classic references where helpful?

### 10. Licensing/originality
Are quotations, figures, and code use appropriate?

## Review Result

Use one of:

- APPROVE
- APPROVE WITH MINOR FIXES
- MAJOR REVISION
- REJECT

## Major Revision Output

A major-revision review should state:

1. problem;
2. why it matters;
3. evidence;
4. exact revision required;
5. acceptance test;
6. a copy-ready rework prompt for the researcher AI.
